What This Deal Is
  • Franchised mosquito and general pest-control business in Southern Maine.
  • Original offering was $2.4M; current LOI framework is up to $2.25M total consideration.
  • Proposed structure: $1.75M cash at close, $300k seller note, $200k holdback, and about $200k working capital.
Why It Is Attractive
  • Roughly 3,600 total customers and about 2,875 recurring customers.
  • About 80% recurring contractual relationship and about 82% renewal rate.
  • 20+ trucks/vans, 40+ year-round and seasonal employees, and a local management bench.
  • Mosquito Squad Plus / general pest expansion could be useful after base economics are verified.
Why It Is Risky
  • Decor/Hanson Ventures shared-resource entanglement can distort standalone economics and operations.
  • Semi-remote ownership depends on Scott, Craig, key technicians, licensing continuity, and Erik's transition.
  • Franchise transfer, lease/facility continuity, working capital, deferred revenue, and seasonal obligations are not cleared.
  • ROBS/legal and household reserve posture need resolution; default household cash is below the $35k hard floor.
Current Proposed LOI Structure
  • $1.75M cash at close funded by ROBS plus senior debt.
  • $300k seller note; standby and interest-only treatment must be confirmed.
  • $200k holdback/deferred consideration excluded from close-funded sources, senior debt, and debt service unless explicitly changed.
  • $260k ROBS default; $300k what-if should be flagged against retirement concentration guardrails.
Verify Before Going Deeper
  • Normalized SDE against tax returns, P&Ls, payroll, add-backs, and bank statements.
  • Standalone Mosquito economics separated from Decor/shared resources.
  • Manager retention, Erik transition, licensing/Master Applicator, franchise transfer, lease, and working-capital schedules.
  • Household cash plan that preserves at least $35k and preferably moves toward $60k.
Lender Must Answer
  • Is $1.79M senior debt financeable with $260k ROBS and $0 household cash contribution?
  • How will the lender treat the $300k seller note, standby/interest-only period, and $200k holdback?
  • What working-capital target, equity injection, reserve, or seller-note subordination is required?
  • Is the ROBS amount acceptable at roughly 45% of retirement assets, and is the $300k what-if too aggressive?
Seller Must Answer
  • What exactly is included or excluded from Decor/Hanson shared employees, vehicles, facilities, systems, and overhead?
  • Will Scott, Craig, and key managers sign retention packages with clear authority and compensation?
  • What transition will Erik commit to, and what happens if he is unavailable?
  • How are deferred revenue, prepaids, AR/AP, payroll, chemical inventory, and seasonal obligations treated at close?
Recommended Next Step

Send the current structure to the lender first. If the lender cannot support the structure without consuming household cash or pushing ROBS beyond the guardrail, slow down or renegotiate before spending more time on exclusivity.

In parallel, ask the seller for the evidence needed to clear Decor, manager retention, Erik transition, licensing, franchise transfer, lease, working capital, and standalone SDE gates.
