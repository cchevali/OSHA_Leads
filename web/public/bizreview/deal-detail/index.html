buyer sequencing rule + buyer-signed LOI

If results weaken after closing

Closing rules lower the risk. These steps limit damage if one part of the business starts to fail. Final warning levels must be set before closing.

Customers and routes

Warning: Revenue, renewals, or labor miss plan for two months.

Act now: Call at-risk customers, fix prices and routes, and pause optional spending.

Fallback: Combine weak routes and cut overhead while protecting core service.

Cash and loan payments

Warning: The 13-week cash plan shows a shortfall or loan payments are getting tight.

Act now: Stop owner draws, optional hiring, and nonessential purchases. Review cash every week.

Fallback: Contact the lender early. Use only relief allowed by signed note terms. If cash will not recover, seek outside capital or sell early.

Leaders and winter staffing

Warning: A key manager leaves, goals miss for two months, or winter staffing costs exceed plan.

Act now: Use backup leaders, Erik's handoff, closer weekly reviews, and controller support.

Fallback: Use an interim operator if affordable and reduce workload or winter overhead until stable.

Family boundary and exit

Warning: Recovery would need household cash, a new guarantee, or more retirement money.

Act now: Do not add family money. Pause for independent lender, legal, and planner review.

Fallback: Seek outside capital, sell early, or begin an orderly wind-down before losses grow. These are options, not promises.

Why this cap exists

What the $10,500 buys

  1. 1. A decision. The current verification window answers one question: do the corrected standalone numbers and seller acceptance justify restarting lender and definitive-agreement work? The output is stop, reprice, pause, or continue, in writing.
  2. 2. An independent household-risk review. Before ROBS, a personal guarantee, or closing is even considered, a flat-fee fiduciary CFP tests what this deal would do to our finances if it fails. That review reports to us, not to the deal.

What it does not buy

Progress toward closing. Every dollar here can be spent and the deal can still die. That is a designed outcome, not a failure — a confident, documented "stop" is one of the two things this money exists to purchase.

The sunk-cost rule

All of this money is unrecoverable the moment it's spent, whether we continue or stop. We accept that in advance, and we bind it: spent money is excluded from every future decision and will never be used as an argument to continue. Same logic as the walk-away triggers — the rule is written now, while it costs nothing to honor.

Proportion, both directions

The $10,500 cap limits review costs before closing. It does not authorize a purchase, loan, guarantee, or added ROBS.
