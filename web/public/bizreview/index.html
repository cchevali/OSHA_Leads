Where this stands

We found a pest-control business in Maine that might be worth buying someday. Before that could ever happen, we have to prove what it actually earns — its numbers are currently tangled with the seller's related operations — Christmas Decor (holiday lighting) and One Hour (heating and air).

Today we are not deciding whether to buy anything. We're deciding whether to spend a capped amount to get answers.

Why this one earned a closer look

  • ·It has run for 14 years and served about 2,875 recurring customers last season. Most revenue comes from customers who renew each year, not one-off jobs. (seller's numbers)
  • ·77.8% of customers renewed this year even after a price increase — above the 74.1% average across this franchise system. (seller's numbers)
  • ·A long-tenured team runs daily operations, which is what would make owning it from Virginia possible. (what we've been told — nothing signed)

What could still kill it

  • ·About $1.5M of the company's $1.7M in reported assets are loans to the seller's two related operations — a holiday-lighting business and an HVAC business. Its earnings can't be trusted until they're untangled. This is the main thing the $3,000 buys.
  • ·The two managers who actually run it haven't signed anything about staying.
  • ·Vehicle costs roughly doubled last year with no explanation yet.

Why that's worth up to $10,500 to find out

Every promising number above comes from the seller. The $10,500 buys the answer to one question: are the earnings real once the seller's other operations are stripped out? If yes, the next conversation is worth having — a 14-year recurring-revenue business at a fair price. If no, we stop, and the loss is capped. Either answer is worth paying for.
